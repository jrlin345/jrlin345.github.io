Optimized Bike Calipers

3D-printed bike calipers optimized using FEA and Topology Optimization

Overview

As part of MECH-ENG 240, Introduction to Design and Manufacturing, our team of 5 was tasked with designing and optimizing a pair of bike calipers.

As part of the class, we optimized the bike calipers using Siemenes NX FEA and topology optimization to match design specifications produced through market and client research. In the end, we produced bike calipers that performed to our ideal margins and successfully stopped a bike.

Step 1: Defining Needs and Basic Calculations

Our first task when designing was to do basic research in defining our needs to meet both industry standards and consumer needs. We researched ISO standards and did Force-Torque analsysis to decide on the needs as well as polling various users

Before generating concepts, the team also established a maximum design space constrained by wheel clearance, frame geometry, and mounting points. To minimize bending loads and stress concentrations, direct load paths were prioritized between mounting points, with smooth edge blends applied at transition zones to prevent localized stress buildup.

Step 2: Design and Optimization

FEA was conducted in Siemens NX under 180 N cable tension and 216 N friction loading. Initial results showed maximum Von Mises stresses of 41.70 MPa and 38.36 MPa for the left and right calipers respectively both within the 42 MPa allowable stress limit derived from Nylon-12's 50 MPa yield strength and a factor of safety of 1.2. Maximum deformation was identified at the cable mounting point, with the left caliper experiencing 5.4 mm of deflection, flagged as a risk for loss of clamping force and increased braking distance.

Topology optimization revealed that integrating I-beam profiles along the middle exterior sections would improve the stiffness-to-weight ratio while reducing deflection. After implementing I-beams, increasing overall caliper thickness, and adding a support beam to the left caliper to counteract rotational deflection, final FEA showed significant improvement — right caliper deflection reduced from 8.1 mm to 5.2 mm, left caliper from 5.4 mm to 2.7 mm, and right caliper peak stress reduced from 61 MPa to 38 MPa.

Step 3: Testing and Analysis

Calipers were 3D printed in Nylon-12 and physically tested on a bicycle. Between the first and final iterations:

  • Braking distance improved by 63% from 29 meters to 10.6 meters, meeting the 15-meter marginal target
  • Braking time improved by 42% from 3.4 seconds to 1.96 seconds, meeting the 3.26-second marginal target
  • Clamping force increased from 75 N to 150 N, approaching the 180 N target
  • Total caliper weight remained well below the 165-gram marginal limit at 69.9 grams less than half the industry standard

Physical testing also revealed real-world loading conditions that differed from simulation assumptions particularly torsional loading on the right caliper due to horizontal force offset at the brake hole leading to targeted geometry changes that resolved the issue in the final iteration.
